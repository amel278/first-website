Snow

Snow is a solid form of water—or precipitation—consisting of soft, white ice crystals that resemble cotton. Snow forms in clouds when the air temperature drops below the freezing point of 0° Celsius (32° Fahrenheit), causing water vapor to turn directly into ice and fall to the ground.

The Process of Snow Formation:

  1. Water vapor in cold air collects around dust particles.
  2. Temperatures below 0°C turn water vapor into ice crystals.
  3. Ice crystals stick together to form snowflakes.
  4. Heavy flakes fall to the earth if the air remains cold.

Unique Fact:

  • Snow crystals have unique shapes, and no two are alike.
  • In Indonesia, perpetual snow could once be found at Puncak Jaya, Papua.

Components and Process of Snow Formation:

  • Water Vapor: The primary source of snow formation is water molecules (H₂O) in the form of water vapor in the atmosphere.
  • Nucleation Particles (Aerosols): Water vapor requires the presence of tiny airborne particles—such as dust, pollen, or bacteria—to latch onto and begin freezing.
